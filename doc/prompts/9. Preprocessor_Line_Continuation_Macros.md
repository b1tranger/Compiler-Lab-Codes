## 3. Analysis & Key Insights

1. **Preprocessor Directive Boundaries**:
   - In C and C++, preprocessor directives (beginning with `#`, such as `#define`, `#include`) are strictly **line-oriented**.
   - The preprocessor considers a directive completed the moment it encounters a newline character (`\n`).
2. **Line Continuation / Line Splicing**:
   - A trailing backslash (`\`) at the end of a physical line escapes the newline.
   - The compiler's translation phase 2 deletes the backslash and the immediately following newline (`\\\n`), splicing the two physical lines into a single logical line.
3. **Readability & Formatting**:
   - Allows long macros (like `loop(var, start, end, step)`) to be wrapped cleanly across multiple lines or formatted by code formatters (`clang-format`) without breaking the preprocessor.

---

## 4. Compiler Translation Phase & Pitfalls

- **ISO C++ Standard Phase 2**: Line splicing occurs before preprocessing tokens are formed.
- **The "Invisible Trailing Space" Bug**: If any whitespace or tab exists between `\` and the newline, the backslash escapes the space rather than the newline, causing compiler errors like `warning: backslash and newline separated by space` or `stray '\' in program`.
